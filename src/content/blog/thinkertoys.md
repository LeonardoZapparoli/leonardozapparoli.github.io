Try to think of a new color, one you have never seen before. Can you picture it?

Whether this is philosophically possible is a deeper question than I'll take up here (see [this](http://radgeek.com/gt/2004/10/29/HowIGotTheBlue.pdf) essay for a discussion), but I think we can all agree that it isn't easy.

Finding it impossible to imagine a truly new color led me to a hypothesis: human creativity is nothing more than the recombination of pre-existing sensory impressions. That would explain why we can't conjure a brand new sensory experience like a color. It would be like trying to reach a direction orthogonal to a basis using only the span of that basis.

Whatever its nature, though, creativity is an important human trait. Novel ideas can shed light on unsolved problems. So how can we get better at thinking creatively?

That question led me to *Thinkertoys* by Michael Michalko. Though written primarily for a business audience, the book lays out concrete techniques for thinking creatively. In this post I'll go through those techniques, explain how they work, and give examples of the kinds of ideas they might produce.
